About

I am a master's student at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advised by Dr. Xuyang Wang. My work is on world models for robot manipulation in the underwater setting — building predictors that reason about how a salvage ROV and its grippers interact with an object when vision is poor, contact is unobserved, and the vehicle's dynamics lag behind its commands.

Before that I studied Naval Architecture and Ocean Engineering at Dalian University of Technology, where I worked on bionic amphibious vehicles and linkage-driven grippers.

Research interests

  • World models for embodied AI — object-centric representation, latent prediction under control conditioning, diffusion and flow-matching planners
  • Underwater robot dynamics — ROV–manipulator multibody modelling and grey-box residual correction of hydrodynamic models
  • Underactuated grasping — closed-form kinetostatics and dynamics of linkage grippers, and learning on top of them

Research experience

World-model-based planning for deep-water salvage ROV (C³-JEPA)
Master's thesis project · National Key R&D Program of China (2023YFC2809701) · 2024 – present
  • Benchmarked competing world-model designs — Dreamer-style latent imagination, DIAMOND diffusion models and pure JEPA — before converging on C³-JEPA, a cross-view, control-conditioned and context-extended design.
  • Built a ROS–Gazebo data foundation (experimental pool, domain randomisation, teleoperation GUI) and collected a large synchronised multi-modal dataset across multi-camera vision, proprioception and all 12 thruster / 6 gripper control channels.
  • Designed the observation module (frozen DINOv3-L features, temporal slot attention, SIGReg) and a lightweight object-centric predictor that fuses several camera views under control conditioning.
  • Built a flow-matching diffusion planner with spherical-coordinate encoding and delay-compensated label shifting, plus a block-causal transformer rollout with critic-based trajectory selection.
  • Learned slots isolate the task object and the grippers where unguided self-supervision fails outright; C³-JEPA with the flow-matching planner cuts terminal positioning error by roughly a third and ranks candidate trajectories well above chance.
Underwater perception: visual SLAM and imaging sonar
Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab simulation study · 2026
  • Rebuilt a pool-scale ROV scene in Isaac Sim 4.5 with four synchronised cameras, six AprilTag boards and a turbidity model (visibility, scattering, marine snow, caustics) exposed through a live parameter dashboard, and recorded synchronised video with ground-truth poses.
  • Built an offline visual SLAM chain — tag anchoring, KLT front-end, multi-view triangulation, PnP relocalisation and joint sparse bundle adjustment — reaching 97.1% / 93.3% localisable frames and 3.7 / 4.1 cm median error in clear and turbid water, measured in the tag world frame without ground-truth alignment or scale fitting.
  • Implemented a forward-looking imaging sonar with ray casting, target strength, transmission loss, Rayleigh speckle and acoustic shadow, plus pose-accumulated mapping; target range and position error were 0.25 m and 0.30 m inside the geometric window. The two chains are complementary — vision resolves 0.09 m at 8 m, sonar holds 0.30 m out to 12 m.
Grey-box inverse dynamics for the salvage ROV
Branch study derived from the world-model project · 2024 – present
  • Formulated a physics-informed inverse-dynamics module coupling Fossen's 6-DOF equations with learned hydrodynamic parameters and a gated residual network, recovering thruster commands from observed vehicle motion.
Underactuated salvage gripper: kinetostatics, dynamics and runtime hold-capacity prediction
2024 – present
  • Built a virtual-work-based kinetostatic model of a double four-bar underactuated gripper giving closed-form contact-force and load-capacity solutions, and extended it to dry and immersed hold capacity, including sensitivity to the reading instant, object shape and seabed-assisted grasping.
  • Validated the solver against ADAMS simulation and an experimental gripper prototype; derived the closed-form multibody dynamics via screw theory, resolving closed kinematic loops with non-minimal coordinates and Lagrange multipliers.
  • Coupled the physical model with a parameter network and gated residual correction to absorb contact, friction and unmodelled hydrodynamic effects.
  • Trained a residual contact-dynamics predictor on 20k simulated grasp episodes: 0.49–3.88 mm error over 20–150 ms horizons, against 1.44–63.3 mm for a constant-velocity baseline, across three seeds and held-out splits.
  • Replaced a ten-step autoregressive rollout with a one-shot 100 ms predictor — 2.10 mm against 18.37 mm, 0.75 ms on CPU (8.6× faster) — and reproduced the physical hold/slip criterion on 99.4–99.7% of starts.
